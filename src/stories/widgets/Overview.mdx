# Widgets & Embeds — R&D

<p style={{ margin: '-4px 0 0', fontSize: '13px', fontWeight: 600, color: '#64748b' }}>Last updated Oct 6, 2026 · see the Changelog at the bottom of this page</p>

Eventpipe hotel booking today lives on its own site. This track explores the
other direction: **putting Eventpipe inside a partner’s checkout** as an embedded
widget, so a racer can add a hotel (plus parking and photos) to the same order
they’re already completing, without leaving the event’s ticketing flow.

The first case study is the **2026 San Antonio Spartan Trifecta Weekend**, sold on
**TicketSocket**. We rebuilt Spartan’s event page and checkout pixel-for-pixel as
the host, then embedded a Presto-built widget into a new step of that checkout.
Six concepts compare **where the hotel booking happens** (inline in the checkout,
in a modal over it, or on a separate page after the ticket purchase) and **whose
colors it wears** (Presto vs. Spartan).

## What we were trying to learn

1. **Where does a hotel belong in a ticket checkout?** We added a dedicated
   **Add-ons** step (*Details › Add-ons › Extras › Payment*) with a “Make a
   weekend of it” card: parking pass, photo package and hotel room as toggles.
2. **Inline or modal?** Does browsing 20 hotels work better expanded in the
   checkout column, or in a focused modal that hands a confirmed stay back?
3. **Native or host-matched?** Should the widget look like Eventpipe (Presto
   navy) or disappear into the host (Spartan red/black)?
4. **One order, two payees.** Can the host total carry the hotel’s *due today*
   amount while showing the *due at check-in* balance clearly?
5. **Can the real library ship inside an embed?** Every hotel surface uses the
   actual Presto components, not lookalikes, inside someone else’s page.
6. **During checkout or after it?** Does a hotel convert better inside the ticket
   checkout (A–D), or as its own booking, offered from the ticket confirmation (E/F)?

**Library components in the widget:** `SearchSummaryBar`, `FilterRail`,
`SortDropdown`, `HotelCardHorizontal` (Group Block “Select Rooms”), `RoomAvailability`,
`HotelMap`, `DsModal`, `HotelDetailPage` (gallery, rooms, amenities, policies),
`CartReview` (hotel order summary), `QPagination`, Quasar toggles and selects.

**Pricing used throughout:** nightly rate × 1.13 taxes & fees. “First night now”
charges one night today and the balance at check-in. For example, Hill Country
Lodge, 2 Queen beds, Nov 20–22: **$122.04 today** or **$244.08 paid in full**.

## Library gaps this surfaced

These are worked around inside the prototype with no library edits, and are worth fixing at the source:

- **`DsModal`** has no ~900px size (sm 440 · md 600 · lg 1040 · xl 1200); the prototype re-sizes `lg` locally.
- **`RoomsCarousel`** doesn’t re-emit `RoomCardReserve`’s `reserve` event, so “Reserve Room” clicks have to be caught by delegation.
- **`HotelMap`** paints price pins with a hard-coded navy, so it can’t be themed, and its event-location pin has no visible label.
- **`FilterRail`**’s inline mini-map uses library sample data, not the caller’s hotels.
- **`CartReview`** clips the “Nights” column at phone width.

## Open questions

- **In checkout vs. after it (E/F):** a separate hotel page keeps the ticket checkout short and lets the hotel be paid on its own, but the racer has to come back for it. How many do?
- **Inline vs. modal:** inline keeps context; the modal gives browsing room and a clear review-then-confirm moment. Which converts better for a single-room add-on?
- **Presto vs. Spartan colors:** host-matched feels seamless but hides that Eventpipe is the hotel provider. Is a **“Powered by Eventpipe”** mark enough?
- **Duplicate add-ons:** Spartan’s checkout already sells its own photo package; the Eventpipe card adds another. Who owns which add-ons?
- **Payment:** the hotel is collected inside the host’s payment step. How does TicketSocket settle the hotel portion with Eventpipe?
